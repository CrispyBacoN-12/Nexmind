## What changed from the 2026-08-16 run

Nothing except the universe. The config (lookback, skip, buckets, seed, iterations, blocks), the
six pre-registered gates and their thresholds, and the underlying price cache are all identical to
`docs/quant/2026-08-16-cross-sectional-momentum-results.md`. The only difference: candidate
selection in `buildSnapshots` is now gated on real point-in-time S&P 500 membership
(`src/lib/backtest/crossSectional/membership.ts`) instead of applying today's S&P 500 list
uniformly across the whole backtest history. This is a data-correctness re-run, not a re-cut of
the hypothesis, using the same reasoning the original study used when it redid its first pass
after finding unadjusted-split contamination in the cache — the original file and its written
verdict are untouched.

This fixes look-ahead inclusion (a symbol ranked as a candidate before it actually joined the
index) and stale inclusion (a symbol kept ranking as a candidate after it actually left). It does
**not** fix omission bias for names that left the S&P 500 before `.cache/bars/sp500-1d.json` was
ever built — those tickers have no cached price history and cannot appear as candidates regardless
of what the membership table says. Every number below remains an upper bound for that reason.

## Data screen (stated before the results)

trusted — same screen as the original study. A symbol is excluded from the study entirely — not
for one month — when any of these holds:

1. its bars are spaced more than 4 calendar days apart at the median — i.e. it is not a daily series;
2. any close is zero or negative;
3. any adjacent-session close ratio falls outside [0.5, 2];

## Reading these numbers

At 113 monthly observations, `t = 2` requires an annual Sharpe near 0.90, while
published momentum decile spreads run 0.5-0.6 over far longer samples. The t-statistic above is
therefore reported for reference and is not one of the gates.

The universe is now point-in-time S&P 500 membership rather than today's list backfilled, which
removes look-ahead inclusion and stale inclusion. It is still survivorship-biased toward names that
have cached price history in the first place — see "What changed" above.

The union calendar holds 2669 trading days. Of the 477 surviving symbols, 28 have no bar on the
first day and 12 have none on the last. The fill/exit logic in buildSnapshots covers those,
and the unfillable count above is the number of selections that could not be measured at all.

## Verdict

**Both legs REJECTED — more decisively than the 2026-08-16 run.** Hand-written after the run
above; `scripts/decile-momentum-study-pointintime-rerun.mts` will destroy this section if re-run.

Gating candidate selection on real point-in-time S&P 500 membership does not rescue cross-sectional
momentum on this universe — it makes the rejection stronger. Leg A's mean spread flips from
+0.236%/month (2026-08-16, backfilled universe) to **-0.071%/month**; Leg B's flips from
+0.118%/month to **-0.125%/month**. Leg B's monotonicity flips sign entirely, from ρ = 0.430 to
ρ = **-0.285**. Gate 3 (cross-definition — the other leg's own spread must be positive), which
passed on both legs in the original run, now fails on both, because both legs' own spreads are
negative. Gate 5 (mega-cap spread) is the only gate either leg still clears; gates 1, 2, 3, 4, and 6
all fail on both legs, versus three failing gates each in the original run.

**The eligible universe did shrink materially, which is the evidence the gate is doing real work.**
Eligible candidates per rebalance now range 349-472 (was 450-476 under today's-list backfill) — a
meaningfully smaller and more volatile pool, especially in the earlier years of the sample, when the
current S&P 500 composition looked less like history. The rebalance count and date range (113
months, 2017-02-28 → 2026-06-30) are unchanged from the original run. That is expected, not evidence
of a no-op gate: `monthEndIndices` derives the set of candidate rebalance dates from the union of
cached bars alone (`alignUniverse`), independent of membership, and `cfg.minEligible = 100` never
binds even after the gate removes 100+ names from some months — so no rebalance date is ever
dropped. The gate's effect shows up in *composition* (who is eligible each month) and in the
resulting numbers, not in *which months* qualify. If the gate were inert, the eligible-per-rebalance
range and every downstream number here would be byte-identical to the 2026-08-16 run; none of them
are.

**This reinforces, not reopens, the 2026-08-16 REJECTED verdict.** That run's rejection already
stood on its own. This point-in-time re-run answers the specific concern the original run left open
— whether backfilling today's S&P 500 list across the whole history was itself manufacturing the
(already too-weak) momentum signal — and the answer is that removing that look-ahead makes the
already-negative case worse, not better. Neither leg approaches NOT REJECTED, so the tradability
question (Webull whole-share sizing at $2-3k) that would follow a NOT REJECTED leg does not arise
here either.

### Consequences

- **Do not proceed to Stage 2** for cross-sectional momentum on this universe, under either
  membership convention. Both the backfilled and point-in-time runs reject.
- **Do not re-cut this hypothesis** on the same data by changing lookback, skip, bucket count, or
  universe. Momentum has now been rejected twice on this cross-section (backfilled 2026-08-17,
  point-in-time 2026-08-18), on top of mean reversion's rejection (2026-08-15, and its own
  point-in-time re-run).
- **The point-in-time membership infrastructure**
  (`src/lib/backtest/crossSectional/membership.ts`, `.cache/sp500-membership.csv`) is the durable
  asset here. It is now wired into both cross-sectional engines and available for any future
  cross-sectional study without re-litigating the look-ahead question.
